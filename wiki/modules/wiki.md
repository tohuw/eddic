# wiki

The wiki module gives a campaign its knowledge architecture: the source layer, the DM-master wiki, page-level visibility, twin pages for progressive reveal, the pen axis saying who writes each page next, the merge flow new lore arrives through, the typed operation log, and the deterministic player projection. It is the substrate every player-facing surface builds on, and the schema a maintaining agent keeps across sessions. It depends on [cli](cli.md) and [lint](lint.md), and its projection output is what downstream surfaces such as [render](render.md) and [publish](publish.md) consume. The module is entirely local and free: markdown files plus a stdlib projection with no paid dependencies.

## Three layers

A campaign under the wiki schema separates raw material, curated master, and generated projection. The `sources/` layer holds the unprocessed input — the owner's and players' own writing, shared documents, and session transcripts — each file carrying `authorship:` frontmatter. The `wiki/` layer is the single maintained master: facts compiled from sources, interlinked in encyclopedia tone, holding the full table truth including secrets. The `dist/player/` layer is the player projection, generated by the projection verb and never edited by hand; every player-facing surface reads only from it. Instruction files (`AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, `README.md`) are treated as non-content and never published.

## Visibility and twin pages

Every wiki page carries `visibility:` frontmatter and fails closed: a page with no marker is DM-only, and only `visibility: player` projects. Where a topic reveals progressively, it is kept as twin pages — a player page under the canonical name holding exactly what the table knows now, and a DM page at the same location with a `.dm.md` suffix holding the full truth. The DM twin may link its player twin; the player twin never links back, because a player page may only link other player pages. This constraint is what makes the firewall mechanically enforceable rather than a matter of vigilance. Assets under `wiki/assets/` project wholesale, so spoiler assets are never placed there; DM assets live under a path containing `.dm`, which never projects. See [Projection and visibility](../concepts/projection-and-visibility.md) and [The firewall](../concepts/the-firewall.md) for the underlying model.

## The projection verb

The module vendors a `project` verb into the campaign's `.eddic/lib/project.py`. It copies pages marked `visibility: player` from the master into the projection directory, preserving the tree, and applies contributor overlays first. What `visibility: player` means is not the verb's own reading of it: the judgment is the shared `visibility_of`, generated into the projection from the same canonical source as the [lint](lint.md)'s and the [constellation](constellation.md)'s, so the page the linter calls DM-only is exactly the page the projection withholds. Its defining property is that no agent judgment enters the file anywhere: the firewall is checked before a single byte is written, and any breach refuses the whole projection all-or-nothing. A breach is a player-visible page linking a page that is not player-visible, does not exist, or escapes the wiki root — a link that would either leak or lie once in players' hands. Contributor material lives under `contribs/<id>/`; a contrib file carrying `replaces:` shadows a base page on every built surface while the base stays untouched in the tree, so removing a contributor is deterministic. Two contribs claiming one target, or a contrib landing on a base page without declaring `replaces:`, refuses projection rather than guessing. Exit codes are 0 for projected, 1 for refused with breaches listed, 2 for a usage error.

## Attribution and the operation log

Authorship is captured at write time or lost: each source file records a short pseudonymous `authorship:` contributor id, with the id-to-identity roster kept DM-only so real names never enter the wiki. Alongside contributor ids and `transcript` for session captures, the axis carries three honest states: `machine` for pages an agent wrote, `human` for prose a person wrote, and `mixed` for machine prose a human has since reworked in their own words. Only that reworking moves a page to `mixed`. A human who reads a machine-written page and approves it has curated it, not written it, and no approval, preference, or request ever restates the mark — it is a fact about who produced the words, and the sale fence reads it, so a flattering mark costs the owner money. Human-authored prose is never stylistically rewritten — only owner-directed mechanical transforms are permitted, and they are logged. The operation log, named in config and appended to in the wiki directory, records every significant action as `## [YYYY-MM-DD] <type> | <summary>` with the type drawn from a fixed set: `ingest`, `reconcile`, `lint`, `schema`, `witness`, `attribution`, `consent`, `sever`, and `merge`. Entries are append-only and never rewritten, and relative dates are converted to absolute. Contributions, derivation links, and the author's clean-room severance are tracked through this log and the [contribs](contribs.md) module's overlay mechanics.

## The pen axis

Three optional markers say where the pen sits on a page, each defaulting to the honest answer rather than the cautious one. `curation:` is `human` or `agent`, absent meaning `agent`, and records who is answerable for the page as it now stands; it is asserted when the owner says a page is theirs to answer for, never inferred from who wrote it, because inferring one axis from the other is the conflation the pair exists to prevent. `ingest:` is `literal` or `derived`: on a literal page an agent may correct spelling, correct grammar, and turn names into links, and may do nothing else — no rewriting, no summarizing, no tightening — which makes it the right mode wherever the source's wording is itself the point. `lint: off` is the owner's deliberate opt-out from advisory nagging on a page that would otherwise report forever. None of the three reaches the safety tier: the firewall, the attribution machinery, and the structural checks a build depends on run on every page whatever its markers say. See [lint](lint.md) for how the two tiers split and [the design principles](../design/principles.md) for the doctrine behind the axis.

## New lore: propose, adjudicate, merge

Lore arriving from the table lands as its own transient page carrying `proposes-merge-into: <target>.md`, never as a direct edit to the target. The proposal is unadjudicated, so it stays off the player surface — the linter refuses one marked player-visible — and it is held to none of canon's editorial standards while it waits. Where it contradicts what the wiki already says, the agent neither reconciles the two nor picks the better story: the contradiction goes to the DM as a question with provenance on both sides, what the proposal says and where it came from against what the target says and which session or source established it, and only the version the DM chooses ships. On merge the chosen text is folded into the target, the proposal page is deleted, and a `merge` log entry names the proposal, the target, and the choice. The proposal dying on merge is the point: proposals are a queue to work down, not a second archive to maintain.

## Applying the pattern

The pattern stamps the schema and seeds — the campaign `AGENTS.md`, a `CLAUDE.md` stub, and the `index.md`, `index.dm.md`, and log seeds if absent — vendors the projection verb, records the module in the manifest, and creates `sources/`. Adoption of a wiki that predates Eddic adds two judgment-heavy steps: migrating player-safe static assets while rewriting site-rooted links to page-relative ones, and marking visibility page by page with the owner, failing closed so anything not verified as seen in play stays DM-only. The default taxonomy is the stock category set (characters, places, story, concepts, eras, species, sessions, systems), trimmed or extended only when a campaign clearly needs it and logged as a `schema` change. Twin pages are created only for topics that actually reveal progressively; empty twins are never pre-created. Two decision points set where the pen starts: ingest mode defaults to `derived` for pages the agent compiles from sources and `literal` for anything a human wrote, with a whole campaign worth setting to literal only where the owner wants a faithful transcription and accepts the rougher read; recap authorship defaults to machine, the agent writing each session recap from the transcript, while a DM who would rather write their own takes that surface's pen and receives the transcript, the timeline, and the open threads instead of prose. The mark is per page, so mixed tables need no setting, but the sale consequence is worth naming before defaulting past it, since machine-authored recaps never clear the [contribs](contribs.md) fence. The verify harness projects a planted campaign and confirms that player pages and safe assets arrive, DM pages and `.dm` assets are withheld, twins behave, and a planted breach refuses the whole projection without writing.

## The standing party page

Every campaign gets a `party.md`, scaffolded alongside the index. It is
the page a table reads most — "who are we again?" — and it does double
duty: its links to character pages *are* the set of player characters,
so tooling that needs that set reads one page instead of a `pc:` flag
scattered across N character files. `tools/wikilib.py` exposes
`party_pages()` for exactly that read, and the reporter treats the party
page as a reachability root, so a character linked only from the party
is not an orphan. A campaign without one gets an info, never an error:
a table in its first hour has not written it yet.
