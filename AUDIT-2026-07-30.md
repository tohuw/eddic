# Eddic design audit — 2026-07-30

An adversarial review of the whole toolkit: not "does the code do what it
means to" but "is this the right thing to have built." Four independent
passes (core pipeline, live-table cluster, session loop, doctrine) plus
evidence from the one real campaign.

## Status, 2026-07-30 (after the response)

Fixed and pushed: the transcript disclosure and the two-audience privacy
policy (1); the missing ingest middle, with claim-level provenance,
whisper glossary seeding, and off-the-record redaction (2); the asset
fail-open, now reachability-based (7); the sale fence, now disclosure
rather than refusal (4); never-say-better demoted from doctrine to a
per-surface default (5); the launcher deprecated (6). Shipped from the
ideas list: safety tools, the table-questions digest, absent-player
catch-up, prep-as-questions, `eddic upgrade`, and a `fragility:` axis on
every module.

Explicitly kept against my recommendation, at the owner's direction:
streamdeck (there are plans), discord-setup, orlog (an export flow is
wanted), and roadmap #14 (which will not involve SaaS hosting).

Still open: the contribution floor never runs for contributors (CI is
`workflow_dispatch` only); `eddic doctor` remains file-existence only;
the player site is still public with no private option; the recorder is
marked experimental but its rekey death is still unmitigated. Follow-on
ideas are in `IDEAS-2026-07-30.md`.

## Verdict

The centre is excellent and the periphery is where a solo maintainer
drowns. Fail-closed visibility, a deterministic projection that refuses
all-or-nothing, and one generated resolver the lint/projection/graph
cannot fork is a genuine invariant — cheap to hold, hard to rebuild, and
exactly the thing a competent agent would *not* produce unaided. Keep
that if everything else were thrown away.

Three things are wrong at the level of premise rather than execution: a
disclosure gap about where session transcripts go, a hole where the
highest-value step should be, and a rights doctrine that devalues the
project's own output. None is fatal. All are cheaper to fix now than
after anyone else adopts this.

## 1. Three honesty gaps

The docs are unusually candid everywhere except where candour is
inconvenient. That is a pattern worth naming, because the project's
credibility is its main asset.

**The transcript hop is undisclosed.** `wiki/reference/data-controls.md`
has a section called "What actually leaves the campaign." It lists answer
clients, resident bots, and the roster. It does not list the session
transcript. But the recap step has an agent read the *entire verbatim
transcript* to author the wiki — so every session's full table talk,
including health chat, work gripes, and asides that were never about the
game, goes to Claude or ChatGPT. That is the single largest content
disclosure in the system and it is absent from the one document whose
whole job is enumerating disclosures. `site/content/privacy.md` omits it
too. Separately, `lore-bot/templates/bot.py:141-146` ships the last 15
channel messages — including bystanders' — to the provider on every
mention.

Consent is react-gated for *recording* and fail-closed, which is good
work. But consent-to-record is not consent-to-send-to-a-model, and there
is no off-the-record, pause, or redaction facility anywhere. Un-reacting
stops future capture and removes nothing already written. Local audio
retention defaults to "the owner's call"; the cloud plan has a 60-day
lifecycle rule the local path lacks.

**"Unlisted" is doing work that "public" should do.** The player site is
world-readable at a guessable URL (`worker.js:489` serves the site to any
unauthenticated request; the unified host default is on). Protection is a
`noindex` meta tag. Cloudflare Access is an unbuilt deferred decision.
The roadmap's defence — URL-only reachability is low-sensitivity — is
circular: it argues the token is low-sensitivity *because* the site is
public, and never defends the site being public. A home game's campaign
is on the open web and no document says so plainly.

**The recorder is sold as live-proven while a known failure is
unmitigated.** It monkeypatches five private py-cord internals pinned to
2.8.0, against an unmerged upstream PR, on top of Rust MLS bindings, on
top of Discord's rolling DAVE protocol. `epoch` appears in the tree only
inside log strings — the documented permanent-AEAD-death on rekey has no
in-tree mitigation, and it fails silently mid-session. That is four hours
of the table's one weekly night, gone, on a module the capture pattern
describes as proven.

## 2. The missing middle

Transcript → recap and canon is the highest-value transformation in
Eddic. It has no pattern, no template, no verify, and no provenance. The
transcriber punts it ("the wiki pattern's job"); the wiki pattern never
picks it up. Meanwhile `convene` spends 250 lines on when to post a
Discord message *about* the recap, and `streamdeck` ships buttons to
start recording it.

The consequences compound:

- **No claim-level provenance.** `derived-from` is a rights DAG, not
  evidence. The `ingest` log records "session N compiled," not which
  sentence came from which timestamp. The transcript already carries
  `[H:MM:SS] speaker:` lines, so citation is available and simply never
  asked for. The campaign schema even instructs the agent to tell the DM
  "which session or source established it" — information the pipeline
  never records.
- **"Automatic is the point" is therefore unguarded.** A misheard proper
  noun becomes a wiki fact, projects to the player tier, enters the bot
  corpus, and is cited to players with a live link. Nothing between
  whisper's output and a player reading it as canon involves a human or a
  traceable claim.
- **Whisper is never told the campaign's proper nouns.** No
  initial-prompt is passed, and the "Known mishearings" section is
  consumed by no code at all. Invented names are precisely what wrecks
  ASR, and the fix is one parameter.
- **No correction path.** There is no supersede/retcon log type. Wrong
  canon is edited silently with no record it was ever wrong, and the
  freshness routine republishes it.
- **Speaker labels are Discord handles**, which is the real-name leak the
  roster convention exists to prevent — contained today only because
  sources stay DM-side.

## 3. The toolkit has outrun its only user

Twenty modules. The real campaign (`~/Projects/dnd/landofsong`) applies
nine, has 59 wiki pages, 12 sources, and three session recaps.

Its manifest — the mechanism that is supposed to make application
idempotent and upgradable — is stale and incomplete. It records
`atlas 0.1.0`, a module renamed to `constellation` with no migration
path; `wiki 0.3.0` against a repo at 0.4.1; and it omits recorder,
streamdeck, and launcher, all of which are visibly installed. One
campaign commit is literally titled "commit the transcribe verb (left
untracked by the session-2 work)."

So the upgrade story is aspirational: nothing consumes the manifest to
*perform* an upgrade, and the only campaign maintaining one has let it
drift. Ship `eddic upgrade` (diff applied versions against the repo,
report what to re-apply) or stop claiming upgradability.

Related: `modules/CONTRACT.md` says the deterministic floor is what lets
strangers' work merge without a gatekeeper reading every line. The floor
runs from `.githooks/pre-push`, which a contributor will not have
configured, and `ci.yml` is `workflow_dispatch` only. The contribution
model's automated gate does not exist for contributors.

## 4. The rights fence is wrong (I built it two days ago)

`MACHINE_AUTHORED = {"machine", "agent"}` refuses any page marked for
sale. Meanwhile the campaign schema says "pages you write carry
`authorship: machine`" and the pen defaults set recap→machine,
ingest→derived, curation→agent. A default-configured Eddic campaign is
therefore almost entirely unsellable by construction. Eddic's core value
proposition destroys the marketplace's premise, and I made that explicit
in code.

Three reasons the fence is the wrong instrument:

1. **Unprotectable is not unsellable.** Public-domain compilations and
   fact databases are sold routinely. You lose the copyright monopoly,
   not the right to sell.
2. **It contradicts the same document two sections earlier.**
   `principles.md` concedes that human selection, arrangement, and
   substantive revision of machine output can carry protection. A campaign
   wiki *is* a selection-and-arrangement compilation. Refusing at page
   granularity is the wrong granularity for the project's own theory.
3. **It permits selling the raw table talk and refuses the curated
   layer.** `transcript` is absent from `MACHINE_AUTHORED`, so session
   logs clear with table consent while the wiki built from them does not.
   That is an exact inversion of where both the value and the privacy risk
   sit.

Replace refusal with disclosure: keep the unmarked-authorship refusal
(that one is honest — a sale should state who wrote each page), and swap
the machine refusal for a mandatory bundle manifest, headline "N%
machine-authored; machine pages carry no copyright." Same moral content,
no false claim, and a product that can exist. It also dissolves the
incentive problem the doctrine currently creates: the schema tells the
owner "a false mark here is the one that costs you real money," then
refuses on principle to detect, then relies on honour.

## 5. Never-say-better conflates two harms

Unearned secrets and tactical help are different things. Secrets are
handled *structurally* — the player companion cannot see DM pages, by
tier isolation. "Never say what is better" adds nothing to that; it is an
unenforceable social norm bolted onto a working technical firewall, and
its only reliable effect is making Eddic's companion worse than the
ChatGPT tab the player has open anyway.

It costs the most for the least in the learner's primer, which "never
optimizes the build." A genuinely new player asking what to do on their
turn gets an option landscape by design. What makes D&D land for a
newcomer is someone saying "try this, it'll feel great." Keep the rule as
a **default the table can turn off**; drop it as doctrine.

## 6. Cut list

- **streamdeck** — buttons so the DM needn't type `/record-start` in a
  channel they're already in, hard-depending on the least survivable
  module. Delete.
- **launcher** — its headline justification (pinning macOS microphone
  permission) is false: the recorder never opens a mic, audio arrives over
  the Discord voice gateway. 592 lines requiring Xcode CLI tools on a DM's
  Mac, which violates the installation-friction principle outright.
- **orlog** — one campaign's tool generalised prematurely, verified
  against a fake CLI. Demote to a reference doc.
- **discord-setup** — IaC ceremony for eight channels that never drift.
  Its genuinely useful output is a three-line curated bot list.
- **recorder** — demote to experimental, require a parallel Craig
  recording, set a kill date on the upstream PRs. It buys the one thing
  Craig cannot (no third party in the audio path), so don't delete it —
  but stop calling it proven.
- **roadmap #14, eddic.quest subdomain hosting** — cut now. It
  contradicts the transaction arc's own boast of no SaaS and no gateway,
  and adds unbounded ops liability to a solo maintainer already carrying
  twenty modules.

## 7. The one fail-open in a fail-closed system

`project.py` copies everything under `assets/` to the player projection,
filtered only by the substring `.dm` in the path. No lint rule covers
assets. `page_ref` deliberately lets asset links fall through unjudged.
So a DM map dropped in `assets/` ships to a public URL even when no player
page links it, and the safeguard is a sentence in a pattern file telling
the DM not to do that.

Every other secrecy decision here is fail-closed, script-decided, and
lint-audited. Fix: project assets by *reachability* — harvest image links
from projected pages (note the `LINK` regex deliberately excludes images,
so this needs a small addition) and ship exactly those; error on a player
page referencing a missing asset.

Adjacent, lower severity: the lint skips the firewall check entirely when
no page carries `visibility` frontmatter, and `eddic doctor` only checks
that files exist. A half-configured campaign gets a clean lint, a clean
doctor, and a projection of zero pages — three green lights over a wiki
whose firewall was never engaged. The result is a silent no-op rather
than a leak, but it is the failure a new user is most likely to hit.

## 8. Ideas worth building

Ranked by value per unit of work. All derive from data the system already
has, add no authoring burden, and are firewall-safe by construction.

1. **An ingest module with claim-level provenance.** The missing middle,
   done properly: pages derived from a session carry a `sources:` list and
   non-obvious claims carry an anchor (`session-12.md#t=1:14:22`). Lint
   checks anchors resolve. This makes "automatic is the point" safe, gives
   the mishearings list something to bite on, supplies the "which session
   established it" the merge flow already assumes, and turns retcon from an
   unsolved problem into a diff.
2. **"What the table asked this week."** Nothing logs player questions
   today. A DM digest of what players actually asked the bot is gold: it
   shows what they're confused about, what landed, and which hooks they're
   chasing. Zero authoring, derived entirely from existing traffic.
3. **Absent-player catch-up.** Someone misses a session — generate what
   *their character* missed and would plausibly have heard about. Real,
   universal pain; a per-character projection of the recap; firewall-aware
   by construction.
4. **Prep assistance as questions, not prose.** Prep is the DM's actual
   time sink and Eddic does not touch it. "Three threads are dangling, two
   NPCs are due to reappear, this foreshadowing is unpaid — which do you
   want to pull?" That respects the authorship doctrine (it asks rather
   than writes) while hitting the biggest real cost.
5. **Safety tools.** Lines, veils, and an X-card record consulted by the
   DM companion during prep. The project already has strong consent
   culture for recording; content safety is its sibling and is absent.
6. **`eddic upgrade`** and a `fragility:` axis in `module.yaml`. Half the
   tree dies when a vendor moves; the other half is plaintext that outlives
   everything. Users cannot currently tell which is which, and the
   mitigation (a dead module stops rather than breaks, because modules
   vendor independently) is nowhere stated.

## 9. Small true things

- `wiki/index.md:46` says "eleven invariants"; there are twelve since
  principle 11 landed. My error, two days old.
- The four-part pattern anatomy is stated in `CONTRACT.md`,
  `patterns-not-code.md`, and `the-module-contract.md`, then embodied
  twenty more times. The concepts layer is largely principles restated at
  length — which the no-egg-sucking principle exists to prevent.
- Principle 8 ("no agent ever decides what leaks; a build script
  decides") overclaims. The roadmap celebrates the semantic routine
  catching a prose leak the structural check could not — an admission that
  the guarantee is structural only. Scope the principle to links and name
  prose leakage as advisory.
- Installation friction was *relocated onto the agent*, not removed. The
  entry cost is a coding-agent CLI, uv, a git forge, Cloudflare, and a
  Discord dev portal. That narrows the audience to the already-technical
  DM, which is the same narrowing as the time-claim above. The genuinely
  zero-account local tier (wiki + lint + render) is real and is never named
  as a supported stopping point.
