# Ideas for Eddic

Written after the 2026-07-30 audit and the work that followed. Ranked
within each tier by value per unit of work. Everything here is filtered
against the same three tests the good Eddic features pass: it derives
from data the campaign already has, it is firewall-safe by construction,
and it adds no authoring burden. Ideas that fail those are at the bottom
with the reason.

## Tier 1 — small, and I would build them next

### Mark the moment

During a session someone types `!mark` in Discord — or hits a Stream Deck
button — and the timestamp is recorded. When the recap is written, the
agent gets the list of moments the table itself thought mattered.

This is the cheapest large win left. The single hardest judgement in
writing a recap is *what was important*, and right now the agent infers
it from a four-hour transcript in which the funniest exchange and the
plot-critical one look identical. The table already knows, in the moment,
and the knowledge is being discarded. Convene is already in Discord and
the transcript is already timestamped, so this is a command, a file, and
a line in the ingest brief.

Second use, free: a marked moment is the natural anchor for a citation,
and the natural seed for the "previously on" below.

### "Previously on"

A sixty-second read-aloud opener, generated from the last recap and
whatever is pending — three beats, the cliffhanger, and the open
question. Every session in history starts with somebody saying "so…
what happened last time?" and someone else answering badly for four
minutes.

Derives entirely from a page that already exists. The only design work
is register: it is spoken aloud, so it wants short sentences and no
parentheticals, which is not how the wiki writes. A separate template,
not a reformat.

### The NPC voice card

One card per NPC, generated from their page and from what they have
actually said in transcripts: what they want, how they talk, what they
would never say, and three lines they have used at the table. Improvising
a consistent NPC is the hardest live skill in DMing, and consistency is
exactly what a record is good at.

The transcript quotes are what makes this better than a character
summary — the DM gets their *own* past phrasing back, which is the thing
that actually keeps an NPC sounding like themselves across a year.

This is also the strongest Stream Deck surface I can see: a button per
NPC that pulls up the card for the one you are about to play, on the
device already sitting beside you. It answers a real table-time need
rather than saving a `/record-start` keystroke.

### Promises, debts, and unpaid foreshadowing

A standing ledger, extracted from recaps: who owes whom, what was
promised, what was hinted and never paid off. Campaigns run on these and
everyone forgets them — the DM forgets what they foreshadowed, the
players forget what they swore.

The prep-questions facet already *asks* about unpaid foreshadowing, but
nothing records it, so it is inference every time. Make it a marked fact
and the question gets sharper and cheaper. Small schema: a claim can be
marked as a promise or a plant, with a status that closes when it pays
off.

## Tier 2 — larger, and I think worth it

### Per-character knowledge

Today there is one player tier and every player sees the same projection.
But characters know different things, and secrets *between players* are a
significant part of how the game actually plays: the rogue was in the
vault alone, the warlock has a patron nobody knows about.

Issue #21 already records that per-player secrecy is convention rather
than an enforced field. The machinery to fix it exists — visibility is
already a per-page marker resolved by one shared function, and the
retrieval worker already scopes by token. This is that axis gaining
values beyond `player`, and the projection becoming per-character rather
than singular.

It is the biggest genuine feature left in the wiki layer, and it is the
one that would make Eddic serve tables that keep real secrets. It is also
where a mistake is a spoiler, so it wants the same fail-closed treatment
the DM/player split got: unmarked means nobody.

### Adopting a pile of notes

The realistic new user does not have a wiki. They have a Google Doc,
three text files, a Discord channel of lore dumps, and a map. The wiki
pattern's adoption steps assume an existing structured wiki, which is a
population of approximately nobody.

An adoption path that takes a pile of unstructured documents and produces
a first draft of a structured, linked, visibility-marked wiki is the
single biggest thing standing between Eddic and a user who is not already
running one. It is also exactly the work agents are good at and scripts
are not — which makes it a pattern, not a verb.

The honest hard part is visibility: an importer must mark everything
DM-only and make the human walk the reveal, because guessing which of
someone's notes are secret is precisely the guess that must never be
made.

### Downtime and play-by-post

Many tables play between sessions — downtime in a Discord thread, letters
in character, a shopping trip resolved over three days. That is canon,
and Eddic cannot see any of it. The lore bot is already sitting in the
server.

Ingesting a marked thread as a source is the same shape as ingesting a
transcript, and the ingest module now has the machinery: sources,
citations, redaction. The design question is consent and scope — a
channel is not a session, and hoovering a server would be a different and
much worse product than reading one thread someone pointed at.

### Continuity checking

The Warden dies in session four and speaks in session seven. A human
notices this six months later, if ever.

The semantic review already reads for contradictions, but it reads pages
against each other, not the timeline against itself. A continuity pass
over recaps in order — deaths, departures, locations, states — is a
natural extension and a real safety net for a pipeline that now publishes
without review. It is also the first thing that would make Ørlǫg earn its
place: the timeline is exactly the object this check needs.

## Tier 3 — cheap, unglamorous, and overdue

### `eddic export`

One command producing a readable archive of the campaign that depends on
nothing — no Eddic, no vendor, no toolchain. Markdown, assets, and a
plain index.

The durability axis now tells a user which modules will outlive their
vendors. An export makes the promise concrete rather than architectural,
and for a project asking someone to put a decade of worldbuilding into
its hands, "you can always walk away with everything" is worth more than
another feature. Nearly free: the projection already does the hard part.

### Accessibility

The rendered site is simple HTML, which is a good starting position and
worth defending deliberately: a text-size control, a dyslexia-friendly
font toggle, and proper landmarks cost almost nothing on a page this
plain. The constellation is the real gap — it is a visual-only artifact
with no textual equivalent, so a player using a screen reader gets
nothing from it at all. The backlinks panel is most of the answer; it
just needs to be reachable without the graph.

### Table drift

Convene already knows attendance and cadence. Campaigns rarely end in a
decision — they slide from weekly to fortnightly to a group chat that
goes quiet, and the DM often does not see it happening.

A private, DM-only note when cadence changes materially is genuinely
kind. It must be framed with care: this is for the DM's own awareness,
never a report card on players, never anything a player can see, and
never a nag. If it cannot be written without sounding like surveillance,
it should not ship.

## Considered and rejected

- **Character sheet / VTT integration** (D&D Beyond, Roll20, Foundry).
  Huge surface, entirely vendor-bound, and the contract already invites
  it as a community module. Not core work.
- **Dice, initiative, combat tracking.** Solved well by tools people
  already use. Eddic would be a worse fourth option.
- **Loot and inventory.** Same.
- **AI-generated art for pages.** Cheap to add, and it would quietly
  make every campaign look like every other campaign. The wiki is
  someone's world; the pictures should be theirs or absent.
- **Player-facing "what changed" digest.** Tempting — it drives traffic
  to the wiki — but the pitch is that players ask an agent rather than
  browse, and a change feed is a browsing artifact. The question digest
  points the other way and is the better bet.
